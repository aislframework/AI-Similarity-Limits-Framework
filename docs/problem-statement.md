# Problem Statement

## The Current Challenge

Judicial systems worldwide are increasingly facing cases involving AI-generated content (deepfakes), but lack objective tools to evaluate them. Today, decisions are predominantly based on:

- Subjective human perception ("it looks real to me")
- Witness testimony
- Emotional impact rather than technical evidence
- Vague legal concepts such as "recognizability" or "potential for harm"

This approach creates significant problems:
- Legal uncertainty for creators, platforms, and victims
- High risk of inconsistent court rulings
- Difficulty distinguishing between satire, artistic expression, and malicious use
- Growing public distrust in digital media

## Why Subjectivity Fails in the AI Era

With current generative AI technology, deepfakes can achieve extremely high visual and auditory fidelity. Human detectors perform poorly on high-quality synthetic media, and traditional forensic methods are rapidly becoming obsolete.

We need a new paradigm: **objective, measurable, and transparent similarity thresholds** that can be used by courts, platforms, and developers alike.

## Goal of This Framework

To transform deepfake regulation from a purely subjective and emotional process into one grounded in technical standards, while maintaining necessary flexibility for different jurisdictions and contexts.
